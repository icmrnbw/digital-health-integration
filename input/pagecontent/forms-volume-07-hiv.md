Volume 7 is the HIV/AIDS service, run by specialised SPID centres. It covers testing people for HIV (including anonymously), the laboratory that performs those tests, and the long-term follow-up of people found to be positive. Structurally it is a laboratory plus a follow-up clinic, with confidentiality as its defining feature.

### What the HIV service does

HIV care is set apart from ordinary care for two reasons: the testing must be confidential, and a positive result means lifelong follow-up. The work breaks down as:

- Testing. People come to be tested, sometimes anonymously (giving no name), sometimes identified. The visit is registered either way.
- Laboratory confirmation. The specialised lab runs the tests that detect the virus directly (PCR) and measure the immune system's strength (CD4 count).
- Follow-up of positive cases. Someone who tests positive is placed under dispensary observation, much like a chronic patient in [volume 2](forms-volume-02-ambulatory.html), with their treatment and any complicating infections recorded over time.

The reason anonymity matters so much is that fear of being named stops people getting tested, so the system deliberately allows testing without identity.

### How the forms relate

The forms follow the testing pathway. A visit is registered, anonymously or by name (176-178), and an HIV-negative result can be certified for the client (179). A sample goes to the lab, which runs the PCR and CD4 tests and keeps its own sample registers (186, 187, 188-193). A positive result leads into dispensary follow-up, where a control card (175) is opened and antiretroviral and anti-opportunistic-infection medicines are dispensed and recorded (181). The lab forms are the same kind of result documents as in volume 3; the dispensary card is the same kind of long-term record as in volume 2. The new ingredient is confidentiality wrapped around all of it.

### A sample pathway

Imagine a person who is worried after a possible exposure and goes to an anonymous testing office. The office registers the visit without taking a name (176) and sends a sample to the lab, which runs a PCR test (186). The result is positive. The person is counselled and entered into dispensary care; a dispensary card is opened (175) and they begin regular follow-up, including CD4 monitoring (187) to track their immune system over time.
